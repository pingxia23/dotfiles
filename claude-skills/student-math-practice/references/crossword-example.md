## The important difference

The student should always be able to answer:

- Which box or quantity am I finding?
- Which part of the problem should I look at?
- What do I already know?
- What should I do next, and why?

Avoid opening with a page of simplified equations from every row and column. Choose a target first and introduce each clue when it is needed. A correct answer that requires the child to map detached equations back to the puzzle is not the desired solution style.

## Concrete example: Puzzle 2

Keep the original staggered layout and the positions of A–F. These are the five equations represented by that grid:

| Location | Equation |
| --- | --- |
| Top row | 10 × A × B = 490 |
| Middle row | C × 10 × 3 = F |
| Bottom row | D × 10 × E = 450 |
| Left column | A × C × 10 = 210 |
| Right column | B × 10 × E = 630 |

The table describes the example for the author. In the worked solutions, use the grid beside each explanation.

### Pick A and inspect its own clues

Show the untouched puzzle. Outline A. Highlight the top row and left column.

Look across first:

> 10 × A × B = 490. Divide out the known 10: A × B = 49. A could be 1, 7, or 49.

Then look down:

> A × C × 10 = 210. Divide out the known 10: A × C = 21. A could be 1, 3, 7, or 21.

Compare only these two lists:

> A appears in both clues. Keep the numbers on both lists: 1 and 7. These are the only two choices to try.

Do not introduce the bottom row or right column yet. They are not needed for this step.

### Test one candidate and follow the connected clue

Show a fresh copy of the grid with temporary A = 1 and B = 49. Label them as a try, using a distinct text color and a written caption. Highlight the top row and the right column through B.

> Try A = 1. The top row then gives B = 49. Follow B down. B × 10 × E = 630, so B × E = 63. With B = 49, we need 49 × E = 63. Count in groups of 49: 49, 98. We skip 63. No positive whole number fits E. Cross out A = 1.

This explains the failure without asking the student to calculate a fraction or perform harder multiplication. The temporary 1 and 49 must disappear from the next grid.

The number-domain rule matters: the rejection is valid because the problem requires positive whole numbers. Do not use this argument for a problem that permits fractions.

### Use the remaining choice, then move box by box

Only A = 7 remains. Explain this before writing 7 in A. Continue checking the choice against the puzzle as the remaining boxes are filled.

| Target | Already filled in the diagram | Highlight | Adjacent explanation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B | A = 7 | Top row; outline B | A × B = 49, so 7 × B = 49. Since 7 × 7 = 49, write 7 in B. |
| C | A = 7, B = 7 | Left column; outline C | 7 × C × 10 = 210. Divide out 10: 7 × C = 21. Write 3 in C. |
| E | A = 7, B = 7, C = 3 | Right column; outline E | 7 × 10 × E = 630. Divide out 10: 7 × E = 63. Write 9 in E. |
| D | A = 7, B = 7, C = 3, E = 9 | Bottom row; outline D | D × 10 × 9 = 450. Divide out 10: D × 9 = 45. Write 5 in D. |
| F | A = 7, B = 7, C = 3, D = 5, E = 9 | Middle row; outline F | 3 × 10 = 30; 30 × 3 = 90. Write 90 in F. |

The target can remain blank in the diagram while the adjacent instruction tells the child what to write; the next diagram must carry the newly established value forward. Do not fill all boxes while explaining only the first one.

### Finish with the complete puzzle

Show A = 7, B = 7, C = 3, D = 5, E = 9, F = 90 in their original boxes. Beside it, check all five equations:

- Top row: 10 × 7 × 7 = 490.
- Middle row: 3 × 10 × 3 = 90.
- Bottom row: 5 × 10 × 9 = 450.
- Left column: 7 × 3 × 10 = 210.
- Right column: 7 × 10 × 9 = 630.

## Visual conventions

The reference uses pale yellow for the current row, pale blue for the current column, and pale purple where both meet. An orange outline marks the target. Temporary numbers are orange and explicitly labeled as a try. Established answers use dark bold text; completed answer cells have a pale green fill.

Keep the original problem visible beside every reasoning step. Use a short legend and name the row or column in the text. The labels and outlines must communicate the meaning even without color.

More pages are acceptable when they make the explanation easier to follow. Keep each complete step on one page with its diagram; do not shrink the text to preserve the reference's page count. Always deliver the practice and solutions together unless the user asks otherwise.
